## 1. Complete Guide to Commercial Restroom Cubicles in India

**Slug:** `commercial-restroom-cubicles-india-guide`  
**SEO title:** Commercial Restroom Cubicles in India: Complete Guide  
**Meta description:** Plan commercial restroom cubicles in India by material, profile, hardware, mounting, application, dimensions and site conditions.

**Related phrases:** toilet cubicle, washroom cubicle, commercial toilet partition  
**Internal links:** Restroom Cubicles, Materials, Hardware & Profiles, Applications, Request a Quote

Commercial restroom cubicles are coordinated systems, not simply panels placed between toilets. Panel grade, profile or support construction, hardware, mounting condition, dimensions and site interfaces all affect how the completed washroom functions. A useful specification therefore begins with the building and its users, not with a finish selection.

### Begin with the application

A corporate office, school, hospital, airport, factory and hospitality property can have very different usage patterns. Consider expected footfall, cleaning frequency, privacy, accessibility, supervision, wet-area exposure and maintenance access. These factors help define the right system direction before details are finalised.

### Select a suitable system family

CubiclePro offers aluminium, stainless-steel, box-up, floating and ceiling-hung system directions. Titan Black uses black powder-coated aluminium profiles with nylon hardware. Nova uses anodised aluminium profiles with SS 316 hardware. Supernova and Supernova+ provide stainless-steel system options. Base Box and Base Box Pro use a shoe-box support approach, while Float and Sky Hung create leg-free floor expressions through different support configurations.

The appearance of two systems may be similar, but their fixing and support conditions are not automatically interchangeable. The selected system must match the approved layout and the available floor, wall or ceiling interfaces.

### Confirm the panel specification

Compact HPL and BWP-FR High-Density Board are different material categories. Their suitability depends on the selected grade, application and approved specification. Typical panel thicknesses offered are 12 mm and 18 mm. Other suitable thicknesses are available depending on project requirement and approved specification.

Do not assume that every grade carries identical moisture, fire, impact or warranty performance. Certificates and manufacturer terms must be checked for the exact selected material.

### Coordinate hardware and profiles

Locks, indicator hardware, door knobs, hinges, legs or supports, wall channels, door-stopper channels and top rails must be coordinated as one system. Hardware visible from the public side should be shown accurately, while internal hinges and coat hooks remain internal in system visuals.

### Measure before manufacture

Record finished wall-to-wall dimensions, floor levels, wall build-up, ceiling level, service routes, door swings, accessible requirements and circulation. Final production should follow an approved drawing rather than an informal site dimension.

### Ask for a project-specific quotation

An accurate quote needs the project city, application, approximate cubicle count, preferred system, drawings if available and the expected installation scope. Final material grade, panel thickness, hardware and configuration are confirmed against the approved project specification.

## 2. Compact HPL vs BWP-FR High-Density Board

**Slug:** `compact-hpl-vs-bwp-fr-high-density-board`  
**SEO title:** Compact HPL vs BWP-FR High-Density Board  
**Meta description:** Compare Compact HPL and BWP-FR High-Density Board for washroom cubicles without confusing material grades or performance claims.  
**H1:** Compact HPL vs BWP-FR High-Density Board  
**Primary keyword:** Compact HPL vs BWP-FR board  
**Related phrases:** HPL toilet cubicle, washroom partition board, wet-area cubicle material  
**Internal links:** Materials, Restroom Cubicles, Pro Doors, Technical Enquiry

Compact HPL and BWP-FR High-Density Board can both form part of a commercial washroom specification, but they are not the same material and their properties should never be mixed. Selection must be based on the exact grade, intended application, fixing method and supporting documentation.

### What is Compact HPL?

Compact HPL is a dense, self-supporting laminate panel produced under heat and pressure. Suitable grades are widely used for restroom cubicles, partitions, lockers, worktops and other demanding interior surfaces. Depending on the approved grade, relevant characteristics may include moisture resistance, impact resistance, stain resistance, scratch resistance and ease of cleaning.

Fire performance, hygiene claims and applicable standards vary by product grade. They should be published only when the certificate for the selected panel supports them.

### What is BWP-FR High-Density Board?

BWP-FR High-Density Board combines a high-density fibre structure with boiling-water-proof bonding and fire-retardant characteristics in the applicable grade. Verified grade information may also cover high density, dimensional stability, termite and borer resistance, bonding strength and screw-holding performance.

The term BWP-FR does not remove the need to check edge detailing, surface finish, fixing design and the intended wet-area condition. Product-specific technical documentation remains part of approval.

### How should the two materials be compared?

Compare the following project requirements:

- direct or occasional water exposure
- panel edge and surface requirements
- impact and traffic level
- profile and hardware compatibility
- desired finish and architectural expression
- fire-performance requirement
- fixing, screw-holding and fabrication needs
- maintenance routine
- warranty documentation for the selected grade

Avoid deciding solely by a single feature. A high-density board and a compact laminate behave differently during fabrication and installation, even when both are considered for washroom-related work.

### What thickness should be used?

Typical panel thicknesses offered are 12 mm and 18 mm. Other suitable thicknesses are available depending on project requirement and approved specification. Locker components, doors, dividers and support panels may use different thicknesses when the approved product schedule requires them.

### The correct specification approach

Name the material category, exact approved grade, thickness, finish, edge treatment, support system, hardware and application. Attach the applicable technical information rather than using a general material name as proof of every property.

## 4. Aluminium, Stainless-Steel and Nylon Cubicle Hardware Explained

**Slug:** `cubicle-hardware-profiles-explained`  
**SEO title:** Toilet Cubicle Hardware and Profiles Explained

**Primary keyword:** toilet cubicle hardware

Hardware and profiles define how a toilet cubicle opens, closes, aligns and connects to the building. They also shape its visual character. Each CubiclePro product therefore carries its own approved combination; a material or grade from one system must not be silently applied to another.

### Profile components

Common profile components include wall channels, F-channels, door-stopper channels and top rails. Their role can include receiving panels, finishing exposed edges, controlling the door stop and tying the elevation together. The exact component set depends on the chosen system.

CubiclePro profile families include black powder-coated aluminium, anodised aluminium and stainless-steel options. Shoe-box, floating and ceiling-hung systems add their own support components.

### Door hardware

A coordinated door normally requires a lockset with occupancy indicator, a separate door knob, hinges and the specified support or leg arrangement. The occupancy indicator communicates status; the knob gives the user a clear external grip. Both should appear where the approved system requires them.

Hinges and coat hooks belong on the inside and should not be incorrectly shown on the public face of the door. A coat hook must never be represented as an outside fitting.

### Nylon hardware

Nylon hardware is part of the Titan Black configuration and the approved Pro Doors specification. It provides a distinct black component language. Use it only where the relevant product record specifies it.

### Stainless-steel hardware

SS 316 hardware is used across several CubiclePro systems, including Nova, Supernova, Supernova+, Base Box, Base Box Pro, Float and Sky Hung. Profile or support grade may still differ between those products, so “stainless hardware” is not a complete system description.

### What a hardware schedule should record

- lock and occupancy indicator
- separate door knob or pull component
- hinge type and quantity
- internal coat hook where required
- leg, pedestal, shoe-box or suspended support
- wall and edge profiles
- top rail where applicable
- material grade and finish
- fixing requirements

Final hardware and profile configuration must match the approved product and project schedule.

## 5. Floor-Mounted, Box-Up, Floating and Ceiling-Hung Cubicles

**Slug:** `toilet-cubicle-mounting-systems-guide`  
**SEO title:** Toilet Cubicle Mounting Systems Guide  
**Meta description:** Compare conventional, shoe-box, floating and ceiling-hung toilet cubicle mounting systems by appearance and site interface.

**Primary keyword:** toilet cubicle mounting systems  
**Related phrases:** floating toilet cubicle, ceiling-hung toilet cubicle, box-up cubicle  
**Internal links:** Base Box, Base Box Pro, Float, Sky Hung, Technical Enquiry

Mounting determines how a cubicle reaches the floor, wall or ceiling. It affects cleaning access, visual weight and the coordination required with the building. The correct choice depends on verified support conditions rather than appearance alone.

### Conventional floor-supported systems

Many commercial cubicles use floor-connected legs or pedestals together with wall profiles and a top rail. They are suitable where the finished floor and wall interfaces can accept the approved fixing arrangement. Final leg location, floor penetrations and water-proofing coordination must be reviewed before installation.

### Shoe-box systems

A shoe-box creates a continuous base expression rather than separate visible legs. Base Box uses the shoe-box condition without a top rail. Base Box Pro combines the shoe-box base with a defined top-rail condition. Their elevations may look related, but their approved support descriptions differ.

### Wall-to-wall floating systems

Float creates a clear floor gap through a wall-to-wall floating arrangement. This can simplify the visual floor line, but it also makes wall condition, span and bracket coordination especially important. It must not be treated as a generic leg-free version of another system.

### Ceiling-hung systems

Sky Hung coordinates support from the ceiling and keeps the floor leg-free. Ceiling structure, service congestion, access, levels and load-transfer strategy must be reviewed by the relevant project professionals before approval. A suspended visual does not itself confirm structural suitability.

### How to choose

Compare:

- floor, wall and ceiling conditions
- cleaning and maintenance access
- privacy and floor-gap requirements
- desired architectural expression
- span and cubicle layout
- service routes and concealed obstructions
- programme and installation sequencing
- approved technical responsibility

The final mounting details should be represented in the approved shop drawing and system schedule.

**CTA:** Share the room dimensions and available floor, wall and ceiling information for a project-specific mounting discussion.

## 6. Toilet Cubicle Planning and Site-Measurement Checklist

**Slug:** `toilet-cubicle-site-measurement-checklist`  
**SEO title:** Toilet Cubicle Site-Measurement Checklist  
**Meta description:** Use this practical site checklist before detailing commercial toilet cubicles, doors, partitions, profiles and supports.  
**H1:** Toilet Cubicle Planning and Site-Measurement Checklist  
**Primary keyword:** toilet cubicle measurement checklist  
**Related phrases:** washroom cubicle dimensions, toilet partition planning, cubicle RFQ  
**Internal links:** Request a Quote, Technical Enquiry, Products, Applications

Accurate cubicle detailing begins with finished-site information. A single room width is rarely enough. Walls may be out of square, floor levels can vary and concealed services may affect fixing zones. Recording these conditions early reduces assumptions during quotation and installation.

### Room and opening dimensions

Record the finished internal width and depth at more than one point. Note columns, ducts, nibs, ledges, skirting and wall projections. Mark the washroom entrance and the direction in which users approach the cubicles.

### Floor condition

Check finished floor levels, slopes, drain positions, floor build-up and any restriction on drilling. Photograph transitions and note whether waterproofing or under-floor services require special coordination.

### Wall condition

Identify the wall type and finished thickness. Record tile, stone, cladding or other finishes. Mark concealed plumbing and electrical routes where known. Wall-supported or floating systems need verified fixing zones.

### Ceiling condition

Record the finished ceiling height, structural support information, service routes, access panels and level changes. This information is essential when a ceiling-hung system is being considered.

### Cubicle layout information

Confirm cubicle count, WC centre lines, divider positions, door swings and any accessible cubicle requirement. The planning image can be used as a reference, but final dimensions must follow the approved project design and applicable requirements.

### Product and finish information

State the preferred CubiclePro system, panel material, typical thickness, profile or support family and hardware direction. If these are undecided, provide the application and design intent so suitable options can be compared.

### What to send with an RFQ

- project name and city
- application type
- approximate quantity
- dimensioned plan or clear hand sketch
- site photographs
- preferred system, if selected
- panel preference
- supply-only or supply-and-install requirement
- target programme
- contact details

The quotation remains subject to final site verification and approved specification.

Junior Cubicle dimensions should never be taken from an unrelated product image or a generic adult layout. The correct proportions depend on age group, supervision, privacy, hardware and the approved school or institutional requirement.

### Use the exact age-band record

CubiclePro maintains four named Junior records: LittleSteps for up to 5 years, Explorer for 5–10 years, Horizon for 11–14 years and Youth for 15 years and above. Each detail page should show only its own approved dimensions and construction information.

### Consider privacy and supervision together

Younger users may require a different balance between privacy and responsible supervision. Older students generally require greater visual privacy. This choice should be made by the institution and design team, not assumed from a single standard image.

### Review door movement

Door width, opening direction, clear circulation and finger-trap risk need deliberate review. Locks and knobs must be reachable by the intended user. Hinges and coat hooks should remain on the inside and be represented accurately in technical visuals.

### Coordinate panel shape and edges

Curved or shaped panels create the visual identity of a Junior Cubicle, but fabrication, edge quality, rigidity and cleaning access still matter. Panel profile should be selected from the approved record or agreed as a custom configuration.

### Plan the complete room

Junior washrooms may also include low-height basins, urinals, UMPs, adult or accessible facilities and teacher or staff provisions. Cubicle planning must work with the entire room, not only the partition elevation.

### Treat dimensions as project data

Published reference dimensions support early planning. Final dimensions must be checked against the intended age group, site measurement, plumbing layout, applicable accessibility provisions and the approved project specification.

## 9. HPL Locker Modules: Tier 1 to Tier 5 and Z-Type

**Slug:** `hpl-locker-modules-tier-guide`  
**SEO title:** HPL Locker Modules: Tier 1 to Tier 5 and Z-Type  
**Meta description:** Compare 1-door to 5-door HPL locker modules and the interlocking Z-Type configuration for commercial projects.  
**H1:** HPL Locker Modules: Tier 1 to Tier 5 and Z-Type  
**Primary keyword:** HPL locker modules  
**Related phrases:** HPL locker, multi-tier locker, Z-Type locker  
**Internal links:** HPL Lockers, Tier 1, Tier 2, Tier 3, Tier 4, Tier 5, Z-Type, Request a Quote

Locker tiers describe the number of individual doors or compartments arranged in one vertical column. They help project teams compare storage capacity and user access without treating every locker bank as the same product.

### Tier 1 — 1-door module

Tier 1 provides one full-height locker in each column. It suits applications that need maximum vertical storage, subject to the approved internal arrangement. Ask for dimensions and internal configuration against the project locker schedule.

### Tier 2 — 2-door module

Tier 2 divides a column into two lockers. It balances compartment count with useful vertical storage and can suit workplaces, staff changing areas and other shared facilities.

### Tier 3 — 3-door module

Tier 3 provides three compartments per column. It is useful when more users require personal storage and the stored items do not need full-height space.

### Tier 4 — 4-door module

Tier 4 increases storage density with four doors per column. Access height and intended contents should be reviewed before confirming the bank layout.

### Tier 5 — 5-door module

Tier 5 provides compact personal-item storage with five doors per column. It should be selected where smaller compartments match the actual user need.

### Z-Type — interlocking module

The Z-Type uses interlocking door and compartment shapes to create a different balance of hanging length and compact storage. It is not simply a decorative door pattern. Final internal arrangement must follow the approved Z-Type detail.

### Custom configurations

Tier 6 and the currently illustrated Z-Type Tier 2 arrangement are treated as custom configurations until complete approved product information is available. The supplied configuration illustration must remain unchanged when displayed.

### What to confirm for every locker bank

- module type and door count
- number of columns and total compartments
- intended users and stored items
- room dimensions and circulation
- locking requirement
- internal shelf or hanging requirement
- base condition, without assuming separate legs
- panel and accessory schedule
- numbering or identification requirement

Custom-built dimensions should be confirmed from site dimensions and client requirements. Do not publish one universal size for every locker.
